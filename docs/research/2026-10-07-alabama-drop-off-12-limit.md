## 1. Statutory text (Ala. Code §38-7-2, current)

**(7) Child-care facility** (the broad term; the license duty attaches to this):
> "A facility established by any person, group of persons, agency, association, or organization, whether established for gain or otherwise, who or which receives or arranges for care or placement of **one or more children**, unrelated to the operator of the facility, apart from the parents, with or without the transfer of the right of custody, in any facility as defined in this chapter, established and maintained for the care of children."

**(4) Day care center:**
> "Any child-care facility receiving **more than 12 children** for daytime care **during all or part of a day**. The term includes, but is not limited to, facilities commonly called 'child-care centers,' 'day nurseries,' 'nursery schools,' 'pre-kindergartens,' 'preschools,' 'kindergartens,' and 'play groups,' with or without stated educational purposes. ... The term does not include any of the following:
> a. Kindergartens or nursery schools or other daytime programs operated by public elementary systems or secondary level school units or institutions of higher learning.
> b. Kindergartens or nursery schools or other daytime programs, with or without stated educational purposes, operating no more than four hours a day **and receiving children younger than lawful school age**.
> c. Kindergartens or nursery schools or other daytime programs operated as a part of a private school and receiving children younger than lawful school age for four hours a day or less ...
> d. Facilities operated for more than four hours a day in connection with a shopping center or service or other similar facility, where **transient children** are cared for temporarily **while parents or custodians of the children are occupied on the premises or are in the immediate vicinity and readily available**. The facilities shall meet local and state fire and health requirements.
> e. Any type of day care center that is conducted on federal government premises.
> f. Special activities programs for children of lawful school age including, but not limited to, athletics, **crafts instruction**, and similar activities conducted on an organized and periodic basis **by civic, charitable, and governmental organizations**, provided local and state fire and health requirements are met."

**(5) Day care home:**
> "A child-care facility which is a family home and which receives not more than six children for care during the day."

**(9) Group day care home:**
> "A child-care facility which is a family home and which receives at least seven but no more than 12 children for care during part of the day where there are at least two adults present and supervising the activities."

**(12) Night care facility:**
> "A child-care facility which is a center or a family home receiving a child or children for care during the night."
> - a. "**Nighttime center.** A facility which is established to receive more than 12 children for nighttime care."
> - b. "Nighttime home. A family home which receives no more than six children for nighttime care."
> - c. "Group nighttime home. A child-care facility which is a family home which receives at least seven but no more than 12 children for nighttime care and where there are at least two adults present and supervising the activities."

**"Drop-in" and "transient" care:**
- The statute has no "drop-in" definition. The only "transient" language is exclusion (d) above, which requires parents on the premises or immediately nearby.
- The regulations mention drop-in only as something a **licensed** center may do: 660-5-26-.08(3)(c) says "If the center accepts children for drop-in care, all of the following conditions shall be met: 1. vacancies in the group ...; 2. staff-child ratio maintained as required; 3. required records maintained and filed; 4. advance arrangement for care made by parent(s)/guardian(s)."

**§38-7-3(a)**, the license duty:
> "No person, group of persons, or corporation may operate or conduct any facility for child care, as defined in this chapter, without being licensed or approved as provided in this chapter."
- The only statutory exemption in §38-7-3 is (b), for church and religious-nonprofit-school programs. (b)(2) adds: "A child-care facility that receives state or federal funds **or is operating for profit** is not exempt from licensure under this subsection."

**§38-7-4:**
> "Any person, group of persons or corporation who or which receives children or arranges for care or placement of one or more children unrelated to the operator shall apply for a license or for approval to operate **one of the types of child-care facilities defined in this chapter**."
- The license designates "the type of child-care facility and ... the number of children to be served **at any one time**."

**Regulations defining "family home"** (660-5-27-.01(14), and again in the 2026 proposed homes rules):
> "A family home is the dwelling in which the applicant/licensee resides."
- 660-5-27 also requires: "Each home licensed shall be the primary residence of the applicant/licensee" and "Child care shall be provided within the family home, not in a separate building."
- So the 7-to-12 "group day care home" category is **not available to a commercial storefront**.

**Regulations on time of day** (660-5-26-.01):
> "day care shall begin no earlier than 5:00 a.m. Nighttime care shall begin at 7:00 p.m."
- "Night Care Facility" is defined as care "after 7:00 p.m."
- "Nighttime Center" is defined as "established to receive more than 12 children for nighttime care after 7:00 p.m."

## 2. The crux: does 12 or fewer exempt a commercial premises, or only reclassify it?

**What the text literally does:**
- The licensing duty (§38-7-3(a), §38-7-4) attaches to a "child-care facility". That term covers **one or more** unrelated children, "whether established for gain or otherwise".
- The numbers (6 / 7 to 12 / more than 12) only sort facilities into *types*.
- For daytime care in a **non-home** setting, the only type the chapter defines is "day care center", which needs more than 12 children.
- So a storefront that takes in 12 or fewer children for care, with parents gone, is literally a "child-care facility" that matches **no licensable type**.

**Two readings:**
1. **Gap reading (what DHR does in practice; the July 2026 in-house view).**
   - DHR's public materials describe only three licensed types: homes with 6 or fewer, group homes with 7 to 12, centers with 13 or more.
   - The Parent's Guide says "Child Care Centers – Caregivers care for thirteen or more children."
   - DHR has no license to issue to a 12-or-under storefront, and it cannot "license" a facility the statute defines out of the center category.
   - Result: the program is unregulated by default, not formally exempt.
2. **Literal reading (the risk).**
   - §38-7-3(a) forbids operating "any facility for child care, as defined in this chapter" without a license or approval.
   - §38-7-16(1) makes it a misdemeanor to operate "as a child-care facility without a license."
   - An aggressive prosecutor could argue that a for-profit storefront caring for unrelated children with parents absent is a child-care facility, and that the lack of a fitting category is the operator's problem: get licensed as a center, at whatever capacity DHR will set.
   - I found **no** Attorney General opinion, case, or DHR letter adopting this reading against a 12-or-under storefront. I also found none rejecting it.

**Bottom line on the crux:** 12 or fewer does **not** create a written exemption. It takes the program out of every defined licensable category. That is a meaningful practical safe harbor, because DHR enforces by category and publicly frames centers as 13 or more. But it rests on a gap, not an express exclusion. Confidence that DHR would treat a 12-or-under storefront as not needing a license: moderate (about 65 to 75%). Only a written DHR determination turns this into certainty.

**What the legislature plainly did not exempt:**
- **Exclusion (f) ("crafts instruction")** is limited to "civic, charitable, and governmental organizations." DHR's own Pink Book (p. 1) confirms it is a status-based exclusion. The fact that it exists implies the legislature regards a **for-profit** crafts program for school-age kids with **more than 12** children as a day care center.
- **Exclusion (b) (four hours or less)** requires "children younger than lawful school age". It is a preschool exclusion. DHR's Pink Book treats it that way, applying (a), (b) and (c) only to programs with young children.
  - **Conflict in DHR's own materials:** the Parent's Guide says, broadly, "In Alabama, child care centers that operate less than four hours a day are not regulated by the Department of Human Resources." That plain-language summary is broader than the statute and should not be relied on for school-age kids.
  - Exclusion (b) also sits only inside the *day care center* definition. Nothing like it appears in the *nighttime center* definition.
- **Exclusion (d) ("transient")** requires parents "occupied on the premises or ... in the immediate vicinity and readily available." It does not fit a drop-off program.
- **The church exemption (§38-7-3(b))** is unavailable: the business is for-profit (§38-7-3(b)(2)).

## 3. Direct answers

**(a) Is 12 the legal limit?**
- In effect, yes. **Thirteen or more children received for care, with parents off-site, makes the studio a "day care center" (§38-7-2(4)) or "nighttime center" (§38-7-2(12)a), which requires a DHR license.** No exclusion fits a for-profit, school-age, parents-absent program.
- 12 is allowed and 13 is not, because the wording is "more than 12".
- 12 or fewer is not an express exemption. It falls outside every licensable type (see §2).

**(b) What is counted?**
- **The statute is ambiguous.** The center definition counts children received "for daytime care during all or part of a day". That can be read as everyone received **that day**, not just those present at one moment.
- The license itself sets the number "to be served at any one time" (§38-7-4; 660-5-26-.08(3)(a), "at any given time"). So DHR administers capacity at one time once a facility is licensed. Whether a facility needs a license in the first place has no "at one time" wording.
- **Conservative reading:** keep the total number of children in drop-off care at 12 or fewer **per day**, not 12 per session. Two back-to-back drop-off sessions of 10 kids each in one day (20 received) could be argued to cross the line.
- Also count every unrelated child present without a parent, including kids at a walk-in table whose parent leaves.
- An evening event that runs past 7:00 p.m. is "nighttime care" under 660-5-26-.01, and the nighttime-center threshold is also "more than 12".

**(c) Does 12 or fewer make them exempt, or just "not a center"?**
- Just "not a center" (and not any other type). See §2. There is no exemption certificate DHR issues for this situation. The only statutory "license-exempt" process is the church/religious one, which is unavailable to a for-profit LLC.

**(d) Is any staff-to-child ratio legally required?**
- **Not for an unlicensed 12-or-under program.** Ratios are imposed only by the licensing standards, which bind licensees, and by the subsidy Pink Book, which binds only facilities taking CCDF subsidy money. No statute sets a ratio for an unregulated program.
- "Two adults present and supervising" is only an element of the *home* definitions, so it is not a rule that binds them. Their "12 minors with at least two adult staff" policy mirrors the group-day-care-home definition exactly. It is a sensible voluntary standard, not a legal requirement.
- **What liability looks like instead:** the standard of care in a negligence suit. Licensed-center rules are the obvious benchmark a plaintiff would cite, and the policy beats them comfortably:
  - Licensed centers: 1:21 for school-age kids under 8 and 1:22 for 8 and older (660-5-26-.04(1)(a)1).
  - Also required at centers: at least two staff aged 18+ with pediatric CPR and First Aid present during all hours (-.04(1)(b)9), and a second adult on premises whenever 7 or more children are present (-.04(1)(b)10).
  - Nighttime centers: two staff aged 18+ on premises whenever children are present (-.09).
  - "Lawful school age" means 5 years old by Sept 1 (660-5-26-.01(16)).
- **Cheap voluntary steps** that line up with the licensed baseline: at least one adult with pediatric CPR and First Aid on duty, both staff 18 or older, and background checks.

**(e) Safest cheap next step: get a written DHR determination.**
- Contact: DHR Child Care Services Division, Licensing Intake, 1-866-528-1694, childcarelicensingintake@dhr.alabama.gov (from DHR's "CCSD Licensing Process", 11-2025). General Child Care Services line: (334) 242-1425. Mail: Alabama DHR, Child Care Services Division, 50 N. Ripley St., P.O. Box 304000, Montgomery AL 36130-4000.
- I found **no formal published "Notification of Exemption" or determination form** for non-church programs. The only exemption paperwork DHR publishes is the church affidavits. Ask by email, and phrase the question narrowly so the written reply is on point.
- Suggested question: "For-profit craft studio, commercial storefront in Madison County, children ages 8 and up, parents not on site, events of 3 hours or less (some end after 7 p.m.), never more than 12 children in a day. Does this require any DHR license? If yes, which category?"
- Keep the reply on file. Even an email reply is strong good-faith evidence.
- Separately, the business-license, fire and health officials already involved in the opening (Madison / Madison County) may have occupancy views on children-only events. That question is local, not DHR.

**(f) Confidence and open questions.**
- **High confidence:**
  - The statutory text and threshold ("more than 12"), as current law last amended 2021 / 2018.
  - That no exclusion fits a for-profit, school-age, parents-absent program.
  - That the 7-to-12 group-home category requires the licensee's own residence.
  - That 13 or more requires a license.
- **Moderate confidence:** that 12 or fewer at a storefront is unregulated in practice. This rests on a gap, with no written DHR, AG or case authority either way.
- **Open questions:**
  1. Whether the count is per day or at one time.
  2. Whether DHR would read "care" to cover a structured, instructor-led craft class. That argument is weakened by exclusion (f), which treats crafts instruction as something that *needs* an exclusion.
  3. Any 2026 session act I could not see. ALISON history shows none.
  4. DHR's broad four-hour statement in the Parent's Guide, which conflicts with the statute.
- **Enforcement:** §38-7-16 makes operating without a required license a misdemeanor: fine of $100 to $1,000 and/or up to 1 year in county jail. Officers of a corporation who knowingly took part can be jailed. 660-5-26-.10(d) says DHR refers unlicensed operations to the Attorney General and District Attorney. I found **no reported enforcement** against a small drop-in, parents'-night-out or craft program. Public enforcement news in Alabama centers on unlicensed home daycares and church-exempt facilities, which drove the 2018 Act.

## 4. Insurance (brief)

- **General liability for drop-off youth programs:**
  - Carriers treat it as a youth-program / child-care class.
  - Standard GL forms commonly **exclude abuse and molestation**, so a separate abuse & molestation (SAM) endorsement is the norm for programs with unaccompanied minors (Insureon, youth programs; Great American Specialty Human Services).
  - Underwriters ask whether the program is licensed or license-exempt, but I found no source saying GL insurers *require* state licensure for a 12-or-under program.
- **Practical point:** describe drop-off events accurately to the agent (Peck-Glasgow). Earlier memory notes the GL application had the wrong operations description. A misdescribed exposure is the bigger coverage risk than licensure.
- **For comparison:** DHR's insurance floor for license-exempt church programs is $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate GL with medical payments. Not binding on them, but a useful benchmark.

## 5. Practical guidance for the copy "Capacity is limited to 12 minors with at least two adult staff present"

- **Keep it.** 12 is the highest number that stays outside the center and nighttime-center definitions, and "two adults" matches the closest analogous category.
- **Tighten what the 12 counts:**
  - make it "**12 children in drop-off care per day**", or never run two drop-off sessions on the same day whose combined headcount exceeds 12;
  - count every unrelated child left without a parent, including siblings and walk-ins.
- **Do not advertise it as "child care"** or "babysitting". §38-7-16(4)-(5) penalize advertising unauthorized services. Framing it as a craft event or workshop is also more accurate.
- **Get the written DHR answer** before relying on any event that could approach 12.
